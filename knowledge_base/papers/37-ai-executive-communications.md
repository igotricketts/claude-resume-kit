# Executive Communications Toolkit

> Codified Florence's design system, strategic accounts voice, and executive copywriting frameworks into a communications skill for client-facing and internal documents, decks, and emails.

Part of the [GTM skill library](../05-gtm-skill-library.md).

## What it does

The skill produces Florence-branded communications for strategic accounts and internal leadership: partnership governance documents, business cases, executive one-pagers, value trees, QBR narratives, contract summaries, steering committee materials, champion enablement documents, proposals, conference follow-ups, account strategy memos, and status updates. Outputs include PowerPoint, Word, PDF, HTML, email, and Slack.

## The three layers

**Design system.** Colors, typography, and visual identity sourced from Florence's design system, so every document matches the brand without manual formatting.

**Strategic accounts voice.** The default voice for external communication: bottom line up front, short declarative sentences, no hedge words, no corporate pleasantries, no em dashes, no emoji, numbered lists for multiple asks, and peer-to-peer positioning. Direct and warm, and honest about Florence's own role when something has gone wrong. Closings carry the sender's name only.

**Copywriting frameworks.** Each communication type maps to a framework from Nate Nasralla's strategic selling work:

| Framework | Used for |
|---|---|
| Situation, Complication, Resolution | Complex decisions, business cases, partnership governance |
| Brief and Brilliant | Scarce executive attention, champions selling internally |
| Selling in Soundbites | Conference outreach, first touches, positioning statements |
| One-page business case | Forwardable documents that travel through the buyer's organization |
| Value Story | Proposals and value trees |
| Future-state press release | Framing the outcome before the project starts |
| 60-second deal review | Internal deal debriefs |

## The buyer's-perspective rule

Documents meant for a buyer's executives are written as if the buyer's own champion were presenting to their leadership. That means the buyer's language (drawn from discovery notes, earnings calls, and internal documents), product features translated into operational outcomes, and a scannable structure with short paragraphs and clear headers.

## Interview angle

"Strategic account communication was inconsistent because every account leader wrote in their own style and formatted by hand. I encoded our design system, our strategic accounts voice, and a set of proven executive copywriting frameworks into one skill, with each document type mapped to the framework that fits it. The rule that mattered most: write every executive document as if the buyer's champion were presenting it to their own leadership."

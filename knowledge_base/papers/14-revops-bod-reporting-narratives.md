## RevOps Board & Executive Reporting: Recurring Narratives and Strategic Framing

This file synthesizes patterns across multiple quarters/years of Board-of-Directors RevOps materials (Q2/Q3 BoD brainstorming, playground, and sandbox decks, spanning roughly 2023-2025) rather than treating each deck as a separate project — capturing the types of outcomes, narratives, and strategic framing repeatedly requested and built for board- and executive-level audiences.

- **Role/context:** Owned (or co-owned, alongside colleagues referenced in deck comments — Andre on formatting/visuals, Jason on plan/pipeline slides, Cooper/Catherine on cash-risk commentary) the RevOps function's recurring board-reporting workstream, translating raw pipeline/bookings data into board-ready narrative and strategic framing on a quarterly cadence.
- **Timeframe:** Recurring quarterly deliverable spanning at least Q2 2023 through Q3 2024/2025 planning cycles
- **What the recurring narrative work covered:**
  - **Plan vs. attainment storytelling:** every deck centers a "Quarterly Bookings vs. Plan/Commit/Best Case" visual with coverage ratios (e.g., "Total: 1.2x", "Commit: 0.9x"), consistently reframing raw bookings numbers into a forward-looking coverage and attainment narrative for the board
  - **Segment-level breakdowns:** consistently split results and pipeline into Strategic Accounts (SA), Site, and SMB, each get their own "win" or "needs work" framing (e.g., "Win: Site Business tracking to hit plan" vs. "Woe: SA Bookings lag affects cash forecast") — explicitly using a win/woe narrative framing device across quarters
  - **YoY pipeline health analysis:** repeated "YOY Pipeline Growth" and "Pipeline Maturity YOY" slides that go beyond headline growth numbers to add a skeptical "better on the surface" layer — decomposing growth into whale deals, new logo vs. expansion, and calling out pipeline that is "not truly accessible" (e.g., large study-bid volume that inflates the topline number without being real near-term bookings)
  - **Market headwind narratives:** recurring inclusion of external, non-Florence-controlled data (industry clinical trial study-start trends from ClinicalTrials.gov, showing multi-year growth/decline cycles tied to COVID funding surges and subsequent market normalization) used to contextualize internal pipeline softness against an industry-wide backdrop
  - **Loss/risk transparency slides:** dedicated slides on loss reasons (e.g., "Site Expansions Loss Reasons"), win-rate and ASP declines, and specific competitive dynamics (CRO bids as ~40% of losses) — framing bad news with root cause rather than omitting it
  - **Pipeline coverage gap and "go-get" modeling:** recurring "pipeline needed by quarter to reach X.Xx coverage" tables broken out by segment, translating a coverage target directly into a dollar-figure ask for each go-forward quarter
  - **Geographic/expansion narratives:** Rest-of-World (RoW) momentum tracking, showing increasing pipeline share against a lower average selling price (ASP) than the US, with country-level pipeline breakdowns
  - **Named deal-level transparency:** "Top 15 H2 Deals" and named large/wildcard deals (e.g., Labcorp, Merck expansion, IQVIA) called out individually within otherwise aggregate board reporting, to give the board visibility into which specific deals carry disproportionate weight in the forecast
  - **Efficiency/productivity metrics:** bookings-per-rep and per-FTE productivity tracking for Site & SMB, explicitly noting methodology caveats (e.g., excluding added BDR/SE headcount cost) to keep the metric clean for board consumption
  - **Top-of-funnel/marketing-ops improvements:** slides translating marketing/SDR process changes (lead handoff redesign, MQL scoring improvements, multi-touch attribution, tooling upgrades) into board-relevant language tied to a quantified H2 goal (e.g., "increase qualification and deal velocity by 10%")
  - **Iterative internal design process:** the brainstorming deck shows the raw idea-generation and prioritization stage before board-ready formatting ("decide which charts/ideas to pursue further and invest more time in design and formatting"), evidencing an internal design/iteration discipline behind the final board narrative
- **Outcome/metrics:**
  - Produced a consistent, recurring quarterly board narrative structure (plan attainment → segment breakdown → pipeline health/coverage → risk/headwind context → named deal and productivity detail) reused and refined across multiple years
  - Narrative discipline of surfacing both "wins" and "woes" with root-cause context, rather than presenting only favorable metrics, across every reporting cycle
- **Skills/tools demonstrated:** Board-level executive communication, revenue operations analytics (pipeline coverage modeling, YoY decomposition, cohort/segment analysis), data storytelling and slide narrative design, cross-functional data sourcing (Salesforce/Tableau, ClinicalTrials.gov industry data, Gong), forecasting and go-get modeling, risk transparency and root-cause framing for executive audiences
- **Resume-ready bullet(s):**
  - Built and continuously refined Florence's recurring board-level RevOps reporting narrative — translating quarterly pipeline/bookings data into segment-level plan-attainment, coverage-gap, and risk-transparency storytelling for the Board of Directors across multiple years
  - Designed a consistent "win/woe" narrative framework for board reporting that paired favorable headline metrics with root-cause context on pipeline gaps, loss reasons, and market headwinds (e.g., industry-wide clinical trial study-start declines), giving the board a credible, decomposed view of revenue health rather than a purely favorable topline

## Worked Narrative: The Strategic Lens Behind Maturing a Complex GTM Organization

This file connects seven otherwise disparate, unrelated GTM RevOps projects — CRO onboarding, the H2 GTM strategic shift, the AE-BDR partnership operating model, event campaign operations, the enablement investment business case, the GTM meeting cadence framework, and the top-of-funnel lead flow diagnostic — into the strategic lens that ties them together: maturing a Go-To-Market organization into one that executes at a complex level across many teams, by pairing alignment work with data-driven problem solving.

### The common thread across all seven projects

Despite covering very different surfaces (org onboarding, cross-team strategy, a two-role partnership model, event infrastructure, a headcount business case, meeting governance, and a funnel diagram), every project follows the same underlying discipline:

1. **Diagnose with data, not opinion.** The AE-BDR deck sized collaboration patterns into named archetypes with estimated prevalence (~20/50/30% split). The enablement case built a five-category, fully-formula-documented cost model. The TOF lead flow diagram tagged every process step by automation type and named the specific breakpoints. The CRO onboarding deck reconciled three competing planning methodologies into one reconciled number. In every case, the starting move is making the current state legible and measurable before proposing a fix.
2. **Name the structural gap plainly, including the uncomfortable parts.** The H2 strategic shift names that "tooling has advanced, strategy has not." The AE-BDR deck admits ~80% of pairs are below the "Strategic" standard. The TOF flow diagram flags that BDRs self-report not circling back on old MQLs. The enablement case quantifies preventable revenue loss in dollars, not adjectives. This isn't diagnosis for its own sake — it's what makes the eventual ask credible.
3. **Design the fix as a system, not a one-time announcement.** Every "resolution" in these decks includes an operating cadence: the AE-BDR model has four standing meetings; the H2 shift has five recurring rhythms; the GTM meeting cadence document is itself a standing reference for how leadership stays aligned weekly/monthly/quarterly; the CRO onboarding deck proposes 12 new operational cadences. Alignment isn't treated as a one-time kickoff — it's built to persist through recurring structure.
4. **Make accountability explicit and enforceable.** The AE-BDR model gates SAO credit and BDR comp on passing five acceptance criteria. The H2 shift asks leadership for three specific approvals plus a 30-day proof-point review. The enablement case ties its ask to protected budget and executive sponsorship, not just headcount. Strategy work in these decks is consistently paired with a mechanism that makes it stick, not just a recommendation.
5. **Connect the tactical to the strategic.** The event campaign ops deck ties a single conference's landing pages and UTM taxonomy all the way through to 90-day ARR attribution. The TOF lead flow diagram ties a single lead-scoring threshold all the way through to Bookable ARR added at Stage 2. Nothing is treated as an isolated operational task — every process is shown in its full chain back to revenue.

### Why this matters as a body of work

Taken together, these seven projects demonstrate the range required to mature a complex, multi-team GTM organization: onboarding a new executive into the data ecosystem, redesigning cross-functional strategy at the leadership level, standardizing a two-role field partnership, building repeatable campaign infrastructure, making a quantified investment case, governing meeting cadence itself, and diagnosing a granular process breakdown at the funnel level. The consistent thread — diagnose with data, name the gap honestly, design a persistent system, enforce accountability, and connect every tactical piece back to revenue — is the actual strategic lens, not any one artifact.

- Diagnosed and redesigned Florence's core GTM alignment mechanisms — from a single lead's automated MQL-to-opportunity flow, to a two-person AE-BDR partnership, to a company-wide ICP narrative spanning four teams — each backed by empirical pattern analysis and a durable operating cadence rather than a one-time initiative

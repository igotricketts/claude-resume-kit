## Published Article: "Doing More with Clinical Trial Business Data: Adopt a Data Warehouse"

- **Role/context:** Authored a peer-reviewed industry article, published on jforcs.com, while serving as Marketing Manager at Bio-Optronics, makers of Clinical Conductor CTMS (used by 1,800+ research organizations worldwide).
- **Timeframe:** Published/visited May 2017 (per article citations)
- **What the article covered:**
  - Made the case for clinical research organizations (hospitals, health systems, academic research organizations, independent sites, site management organizations) to adopt data warehousing rather than relying on siloed, relational research application databases
  - Explained the distinction between a data warehouse, data marts, and rudimentary data repositories, and why the unstructured, query/analysis-oriented design of a data warehouse better serves business intelligence than transactional systems like a CTMS, EMR, IWRS, or EDC alone
  - Outlined specific use cases: business-wide reporting across multiple systems, data standardization across disparate systems (e.g., multiple EMRs with inconsistent field naming), handling reports too large for a single relational database, and supporting multiple data marts for different teams/therapeutic areas/sponsors
  - Detailed business value areas: trial budget negotiation (comparing historical costs by therapeutic area, sponsor, or procedure), trial feasibility assessment (determining potential patient population across multiple EMRs), improved subject enrollment (cited a Boston Medical Center case study showing chart-audit efficiency improving 147% and audit time dropping 91%, contributing to a 240% increase in enrolled subjects), faster audit/regulatory response, and organization-wide risk reduction through standardized data
  - Concluded that data warehousing adoption would continue expanding across the clinical research industry at organizations of all sizes as businesses sought to maximize the value of their trial data
- **Outcome/metrics:** Published, citation-backed industry thought-leadership article referencing five external sources plus a real-world case study (Boston Medical Center)
- **Skills/tools demonstrated:** Technical/industry thought-leadership writing, clinical research technology domain expertise (CTMS, EMR, IWRS, EDC, data warehousing/data marts), research and citation of academic/industry sources, content marketing for a B2B SaaS audience

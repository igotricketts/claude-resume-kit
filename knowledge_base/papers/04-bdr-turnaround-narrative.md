## Worked Narrative: Rapid Assessment as a Leadership Playbook (BDR Org, Florence)

This file connects the Q1 assessment, Q2 evolution, and Enterprise BDR proposal into a single story arc — useful for resume bullets, interview answers, and case-study style writing about leadership approach.

### The arc

1. **Inherit and diagnose fast (March 2026).** Took over an existing BDR team mid-quarter with no transition runway. Rather than waiting a full quarter to "get oriented," ran an immediate audit of pipeline data, activity logging, and reporting — surfacing a $1.7M attribution error and inconsistent rep-level process within the first assessment cycle.
2. **Convert diagnosis into a concrete operating plan.** The Q1 findings weren't just observations — they became a specific action list (standardize tooling, set activity minimums, question territory assumptions, tighten AE/marketing alignment) that the team executed against in Q2.
3. **Prove the plan with QoQ data, and don't cherry-pick the good news.** Q2 results were mixed on the surface (pipeline $ short of target, closed-won down 68% headline). Rather than hide the miss, the Q2 review isolated the one-time NHS deal effect and showed the real underlying trend (-24%, not -85%) — while every individual rep's trajectory improved QoQ. This is the difference between reporting activity and reporting judgment.
4. **Let the data point to the next, bigger problem.** The Q1 deck already flagged a nagging question: "should SA BDRs only work SA?" — i.e., is one playbook being stretched across segments that don't behave the same way? By Q2, the evidence supported taking that question seriously. The result was the Enterprise BDR Evolution proposal: a full role redesign (SADR), new scorecard, and OTE-neutral comp rebuild — moving from incremental team management to structural GTM strategy.

### Why this sequence matters (the "quick assessment → action → structural change" thesis)

- Quick, honest assessment on entry (Q1) prevented months of running an inherited team on autopilot with broken attribution and no shared process.
- Turning that assessment into specific, trackable actions (not just a diagnosis memo) is what made Q2's QoQ gains measurable and attributable to leadership decisions rather than market conditions.
- Being rigorous about *what the data actually shows* (isolating the NHS effect, tracking individual rep trajectories rather than only team aggregates) built the credibility needed to make a much larger ask later — restructuring a role and comp plan for 12 reps.
- The Enterprise proposal wasn't a standalone strategic idea; it's the direct, evidence-backed descendant of a question raised in the very first team assessment. That continuity — from tactical fix to structural redesign — is the strongest resume/interview story here.

### Resume-ready bullets (rolled up across all three decks)

- Inherited an underperforming BDR organization mid-quarter; diagnosed root-cause data and process gaps within weeks (including a $1.7M unattributed pipeline error), driving Q1 SAL attainment to 126% of target
- Converted initial team assessment into a structured action plan that produced measurable QoQ gains (outbound activity up to +266%, opportunities advanced to quote +67%) while accurately isolating one-time effects from underlying trends
- Used performance data from two consecutive quarters to identify a structural mismatch between Florence's BDR motion and its enterprise segment (<30% attainment vs. 88% industry benchmark), authoring an executive proposal that redesigned the role, scorecard, and OTE-neutral compensation for 12 reps
- Demonstrated a repeatable leadership pattern — rapid assessment on entry, disciplined data-driven iteration, and evidence-based structural change — that improved both team-level outcomes and individual rep trajectories within two quarters

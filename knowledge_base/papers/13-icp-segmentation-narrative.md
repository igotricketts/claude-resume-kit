## Worked Narrative: Building Shared GTM Targeting from Scratch (ICP, Tiering, Personas)

This file connects the Sponsor/CRO Tiering, ICP Rollout, and Persona/Buyer-Role work into one story arc — useful for resume bullets and interview answers about building foundational GTM infrastructure where none existed.

### The arc

1. **Start from disparate, non-existent targeting.** Before this work, Florence had no shared definition of a good-fit account, no consistent segmentation, and disparate/ad hoc persona knowledge scattered across teams — marketing, sales, and BDR were effectively targeting independently.
2. **Build the account layer first (Tiering).** The Sponsor/CRO Tiering project mapped the entire addressable universe into a structured six-tier, three-segment framework, closing a real coverage gap (Tier 3 accounts sat at 9% coverage despite holding over half the in-territory account base) and giving every team a shared account map.
3. **Build the qualitative layer on top (ICP).** With accounts mapped, the ICP work defined what "good fit" actually means — synthesizing exec, AE, and industry-expert input into criteria mapped directly to MEDDPICC, so the ICP wasn't just a marketing artifact but a live sales qualification tool.
4. **Build the human layer underneath both (Personas).** The persona and buyer-role work mapped who actually sits inside a target account and what role each person plays in a deal (champion, gatekeeper, financial approver, etc.), turning a raw Salesforce job-title mess into a usable, AI-assisted taxonomy.
5. **The result: one shared language across marketing, sales, and BDR.** Account tier answers "is this the right company." ICP criteria answer "is this the right fit." Personas answer "who do we need to reach and how." Together they replaced three previously disconnected efforts with a single, mutually reinforcing GTM targeting system — and became the direct input layer for Clay's AI-driven enrichment and personalization work.

### Why this sequence matters (the "account, fit, people" thesis)

- Segmentation without a fit definition just tells you who exists, not who to prioritize — the ICP layer was necessary to make tiering actionable.
- An ICP without persona/buying-committee mapping tells reps what a good account looks like but not who inside it to actually talk to — the persona layer closed that gap.
- Each piece was explicitly built to feed the others (tiering data feeds ICP sub-segmentation; personas feed ICP's MEDDPICC mapping; both feed Clay's contact and account enrichment), rather than three siloed initiatives.

### Resume-ready bullets (rolled up across all three documents)

- Built Florence's foundational GTM targeting infrastructure from scratch — account tiering, Ideal Customer Profile, and buyer-persona/buying-committee mapping — replacing disconnected, ad hoc targeting across marketing, sales, and BDR with one shared framework
- Redesigned account segmentation into a 6-tier, 3-segment model spanning 4,648 accounts, closing a structural coverage gap where the largest tier by account count generated the least pipeline
- Defined and rolled out a MEDDPICC-mapped Ideal Customer Profile adopted org-wide, shifting BDR outbound from broad volume to targeted, criteria-driven prioritization
- Directed buyer-persona and stakeholder-role mapping (initiator, champion, decision maker, financial approver) across the clinical-trial buying committee, using AI-assisted classification to turn raw CRM job-title data into a reusable targeting taxonomy

### TH-01 (GTM-Wide Operational Framework): Field Context
**The problem:** Fast-growing mid-market SaaS companies (THREAD's stage during Kyle's tenure) frequently scale headcount faster than process — sales, marketing, BD, and CS each develop their own tools and definitions of success, and revenue forecasting becomes guesswork stitched together from disconnected spreadsheets.
**Competing approaches:** Many companies at this stage either over-invest in enterprise tooling before the org is ready to use it well, or under-invest in RevOps entirely and rely on a single ops generalist doing everything ad hoc.
**Why this matters:** Building GTM-wide operational frameworks (shared definitions, forecasting models, KPI dashboards) at THREAD's stage is a known inflection point — get it right and the org can scale predictably; get it wrong and every subsequent hire inherits chaos.
**Differentiation:** This was Kyle's first department-head-level RevOps build, directly informing the deeper systems and AI work he'd later lead at Florence — a clear "built it once at mid-market scale, then built it again at greater depth and complexity" career arc.

### Field Overview: The Marketing-to-RevOps Career Transition
Kyle's path from Marketing Manager (Bio-Optronics) through Senior Manager, Marketing (THREAD) into Director, Revenue Operations (THREAD) reflects a broader industry pattern: marketing leaders with strong systems/attribution instincts (HubSpot-Salesforce integration, funnel analytics) are increasingly moving into RevOps leadership, since the skill overlap (data-driven, cross-functional, systems-minded) is substantial. This is useful cover-letter framing for roles that want a RevOps leader with genuine marketing-systems fluency, not just a sales-ops background.
